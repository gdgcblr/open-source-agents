# BuildBuddy attendee guide

BuildBuddy helps you find a relevant conversation and gives you a reason to start it. You choose whom to approach; it never sends messages on your behalf.

## Join your event

1. Complete the organizer’s form with your offered skills, learning needs, interests, and project. Choose Yes only if you want your profile used for networking.
2. Get the shared BuildBuddy link, event name, and your private access token from the organizer.
3. Select the event and sign in. Keep your token private; it lets its holder use your profile. Your public badge ID is different.

If you lose your token or think it was exposed, ask the organizer to verify your identity and replace it. Replacing a token invalidates the old one.

## Find a useful conversation

Describe your goal in ordinary language:

- “I want to start contributing to open source.”
- “I want to put my app online.”
- “I’m building a mobile app and need help with the interface.”
- “I want to run AI offline.”

Leave the goal blank to use the needs from your profile. The local matcher searches offered skills, interests, and project descriptions. If the local semantic model is enabled, it also compares meaning rather than requiring exact words.

Each card explains its evidence. **Offers relevant skills** means the person listed a directly relevant skill. **Related offered skills** or **Relevant project** suggests a useful lead. **Shared-interest connection** means a fellow interested participant, not a verified expert or mentor. Open “See the profile evidence” and ask about their actual experience.

“Only matches with relevant offered skills” narrows results when you need practical help. “Only people checked in now” narrows results to recent voluntary check-ins. Your networking-window filter also affects who appears.

If there are no matches, relax a filter or clarify your goal. The app intentionally prefers no match to invented expertise. English requests work best; negation and complex instructions may be misunderstood.

## Find each other in the room

Choose an approved meeting spot, such as Table B, and check in for 15, 30, or 60 minutes. Others may then see that recent location alongside your recommendation. End your check-in when you move or no longer want to be approached.

Use the person’s public badge code to confirm you found the right attendee. “Last checked in” is not a guarantee that they are still there, available, or willing to meet. Ask before starting a conversation.

After meeting someone, click **We’ve met**. They are excluded from your future suggestions. **Show people I’ve already met** clears your exclusion history if you want to include them again.

## Your data and choices

- Matching is local to the organizer’s server by default. Your browser is not downloading the attendee spreadsheet.
- Other authenticated attendees in your event may see your opted-in display name, skills, needs, interests, project, networking window, and active meeting spot.
- The optional Gemini planner checkbox sends your typed goal and aggregate hit counts to Google. It does not send attendee profiles or locations. Leave it off to keep your request local too. Do not type private information into goals or profile fields.
- Sign out on shared devices. Tokens stay in browser memory only, so reloading requires another sign-in.
- **Remove my profile** deletes your BuildBuddy profile, check-in, and associated met history. Your attendee ID remains excluded from future imports for this event.
- Ask the organizer separately to remove your source form response and explain their retention policy. BuildBuddy cannot delete those external copies.

For the fictional sample, choose a profile from “Try a profile” instead of obtaining a token. Sample tokens are public and must never be used for real participants.
