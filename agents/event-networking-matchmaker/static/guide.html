For attendees

  1. Fill the event’s Google Form. Describe what you can offer, what you need, your interests, and your project. Opt in only if you want to appear in networking matches.
  2. Get the BuildBuddy link and your private access token from the organizer. Select your event and sign in. Never share your token or put it on your badge.
  3. Enter your goal in ordinary language, such as “I want to start contributing to open source.” The assistant searches skills, interests, and projects. Leave it blank to use your profile’s needs.
  4. Read the evidence on each match. A shared interest is a reason to talk, not proof of expertise. “Only matches with relevant offered skills” narrows the results when you need practical help.
  5. To be found, choose an approved meeting spot and check in for a short time. Show your public badge code when meeting someone. A location is their last voluntary check-in, not live tracking.
  6. Click “We’ve met” to see someone new. Use “Show people I’ve already met” to clear your exclusion history. End your check-in when you move away.
  7. Sign out on shared devices. “Remove my profile” removes your BuildBuddy profile, check-in, and met history and prevents the same attendee ID being reimported. Ask the organizer separately to delete your original form response.

No results? Remove the available-now filter, try a different time window, or state a more specific goal. The app will not make up an expert just to fill three cards.

For organizers

  1. Run one shared BuildBuddy instance. For real attendees, use HTTPS and a private persistent data directory. Do not run real events in sample mode.
  2. Use the form fields in the project’s ORGANIZER_GUIDE.md. Assign stable attendee IDs in the response sheet and obtain explicit consent to show profile fields to other participants.
  3. Export the response sheet as XLSX. Keep headers in row 1 and use Form Responses 1 as the sheet name, or place it first. Use plain text rather than formulas for attendee fields.
  4. Open “Organizer workspace.” Enter the key from your server configuration, the event name, meeting spots, and the XLSX file. Reimporting the same name replaces the entire profile snapshot.
  5. Download new private access tokens immediately. Give each attendee only their own token after verifying identity. Existing tokens survive reimports. If a token is lost, use “Manage event access” to replace it; the old token becomes invalid.
  6. Put signs at meeting spots and public attendee IDs on badges. Publish the shared app link. Tokens and organizer keys must never appear on public signs.
  7. Update responses and reimport when needed. Missing and opted-out attendees are removed. In-app withdrawals remain excluded even if an old response sheet is uploaded.
  8. After the event, use “Delete event data” in the organizer workspace according to your announced retention policy. Also manage the source form, spreadsheet, token downloads, and backups separately.

What stays private

By default, matching runs on the organizer’s server. The optional local semantic model performs inference without uploading attendee profiles. The basic NLP engine works even without downloaded model weights.

Your display name, skills, needs, interests, project, preferred networking window, and active check-in can be shown to other signed-in attendees at the same event. Email and unknown spreadsheet columns are discarded on import. Never put contact details or sensitive information in public profile fields.

The optional Gemini checkbox sends your typed goal and aggregate result counts to Google. It never sends attendee profiles, badge IDs, tokens, or meeting spots. Leave it off to keep the request local too.

Private access tokens are bearer credentials. Anyone with your token can use your event profile until it is replaced or withdrawn. Ask the organizer to rotate a compromised token. The organizer controls hosting and source-response retention.

Local semantic AI improves language matching. Evidence, consent, and access rules stay enforced by code.
